# Earth imagery and elevations

The imagery and sampled elevation grid use a global equirectangular grid. The west edge is 180° W, the north edge is 90° N, and rows progress south. Geolocations within raster cells refer to their centers.

## Drainage model

The model represents equilibrium drawdown from an ocean-floor opening. The lowest water surface attainable at a site is the highest bed elevation on its lowest-saddle path to the plug, including the plug bed itself. Once the plug's waterline falls below that sill, water in the disconnected basin remains at the sill. The network follows four-neighbor raster connectivity and wraps across the antimeridian. Inland lakes without a present-day path to the ocean are excluded from drainage.

The global model uses GMT 3 arc-minute elevations. Six regional windows resolve passages at 15 arc-second spacing. Gibraltar and the Sicily Channel use mean depths from the European Marine Observation and Data Network (EMODnet) Digital Bathymetry DTM. SRTM15+ v2.7 covers the Turkish straits, the Danish straits, Hormuz, and the Bab el Mandeb–Hanish passage. Within the EMODnet windows, chart depths use Lowest Astronomical Tide as the published vertical datum. Combining these with mean-sea-level terrain leaves a local tidal-datum offset. The published tree preserves minimum-pass elevations on this 29.4-million-node adaptive grid with the three survey constraints below. The display and area and volume estimates sample that network on a 0.25° grid. Spherical cell areas weight the statistics. [EMODnet European bathymetry](https://emodnet.ec.europa.eu/en/bathymetry) · [EMODnet WCS documentation](https://emodnet.ec.europa.eu/en/emodnet-web-service-documentation)

**Independent check.** With a deep North Atlantic plug, the western Mediterranean retains a surface at −291 m. The value comes directly from the EMODnet terrain across Gibraltar, without a prescribed hydraulic connection. An independent connected-component flood calculation on the local source raster also gives −291 m. Published estimates for the controlling Camarinal sill are about −284 to −290 m. [Gibraltar observations](https://doi.org/10.1029/2008JC005023) · [Review of sill estimates](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011RG000376)

**Survey constraints.** Three narrow passages remain too shallow in the gridded source. For each one the graph includes a connection across the channel at its published sill depth. The anchors lie in deeper water on opposite sides. All local seabed elevations remain those of the underlying DEM; these links improve the separation level of neighboring basins. Their thresholds are calibration inputs.

| Passage | Deep-water anchors (lon, lat in degrees) | Applied sill |
|---|---|---|
| Dardanelles, Aegean to Marmara | (25.8, 39.9) to (27.8, 40.7) | −65 m |
| Bosporus, Marmara to Black Sea | (28.9, 40.9) to (29.25, 41.35) | −35 m |
| Hanish–Bab el Mandeb, Red Sea to Gulf of Aden | (42.0, 14.95) to (43.9, 12.35) | −137 m |

The individual basin cutoffs in the complete model reproduce those three input levels. Small isolated basins and unsurveyed straits can still be missed. [Turkish straits research](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0025322709001509) · [Red Sea measurements](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018JC014478) · [Hanish sill depth and uncertainty](https://www.disperse-project.org/sites/disperse-project.org/files/uploads/2011_Lambeck_etal._QSR_RedSea.pdf)

Rebuild the derived grids on a little-endian workstation with GDAL, Python, and a C++17 compiler by running `python3 tools/build-spills.py /tmp/pelagic-relief-cache assets` from this project. The script downloads and retains GMT tiles and the two EMODnet coverage responses in the specified cache. Water removal changes terrain exposure against present-day fixed bedrock. Drawdown speed is illustrative. The model omits weather, river discharge, evaporation, tectonic response, tides, sediment transport, and sea-surface gravity variations.

Marine place names use Natural Earth's public-domain 1:50m geography marine polygons. The boundaries are simplified to 0.075° for regional labeling. Where named areas overlap, the more local mapped feature takes precedence. [Source polygons](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/blob/master/geojson/ne_50m_geography_marine_polys.geojson) · [Natural Earth terms](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/)
