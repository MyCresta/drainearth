Inside the experiments

Drain Plug

Water drains as long as it can reach your plug. When a ridge emerges, the basin behind it keeps its water at the height of the underwater pass. The ocean stops falling when it reaches the plug.

Connections use 3-arcminute global bathymetry, finer 15-arcsecond data around six passages, and three surveyed sill depths. The globe and area estimates are sampled at 0.25° intervals. Small lakes and narrow channels can still be missed.

A deep Atlantic plug isolates the western Mediterranean at a 291 m drop in this model. Published measurements put the controlling sill at Gibraltar around 284–290 m.

Drain time is illustrative. Terrain stays fixed, and the model omits tides, erosion, ice changes and the effects of redistributing water on gravity.

Message in a Bottle

Follow a real drifting buoy for seven days, then mark where you think it was on day 30. Distance from its recorded position determines your score.
